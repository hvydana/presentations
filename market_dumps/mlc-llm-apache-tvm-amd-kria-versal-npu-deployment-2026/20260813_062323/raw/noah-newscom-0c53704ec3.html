AMD extends AI portfolio with Ryzen X100 and Kria SoM for demanding robotics and industrial workloads

AMD unveils the Ryzen AI Embedded X100 family and Kria AI system-on-module, targeting real-time AI processing in robotics, automotive, and industrial sectors with high-performance multi-core and neural processing capabilities.

AMD has added a new layer to its embedded AI line-up with the Ryzen AI Embedded X100 family and a Kria AI system-on-module aimed at robotics and other physical AI workloads, extending a portfolio that the company first introduced in January with the Ryzen AI Embedded P100 and X100 series. AMD says the chips combine Zen 5 CPU cores, RDNA 3.5 graphics and an XDNA 2 neural processing unit on a single device, reflecting its push towards local AI processing in environments where power, heat and board space are tightly constrained. According to AMD, the target uses include automotive cockpits, industrial automation, smart healthcare and autonomous systems.

The new X100 family arrives in six variants, including industrial-grade versions, with top-end models offering 16 CPU cores, boost clocks of up to 5.1GHz and up to 50 TOPS of NPU performance. AMD claims the parts can deliver materially higher multithreaded CPU performance, graphics throughput and token-generation speed than equivalent Intel Core Ultra Series 3 processors, while also comparing favourably with NVIDIA’s Jetson Thor platform and discrete GPU configurations in certain workloads. The chips support LPDDR5X memory, multiple display outputs, PCIe Gen4, USB4 and a wide temperature range on the industrial models, and AMD says they are backed by an open software stack spanning Linux, ROCm, Xen and mainstream AI frameworks.

Sampling of the Ryzen AI Embedded X100 series began in June, with mass production planned for the fourth quarter of 2026. AMD says the family will remain available for purchase until at least 2037, signalling a long support window for industrial buyers. The company is also lining up system-on-module partners including Arbor, Congatec, iBase, IEI, Sapphire and Seavo, suggesting a broad ecosystem around the new platform.

Alongside the processor launch, AMD has unveiled the Kria AI SoM and a Kria AI Robotics Developer Platform built around the X100. The module is described as a COM-HPC design without an FPGA on the module itself, while the carrier board adds an FPGA and a wide array of robotics-oriented interfaces, including camera, Ethernet, CAN-FD, RS485 and USB connectivity. AMD says the platform is intended to support demanding robotic decision-making, with internal test claims pointing to hundreds of concurrent agents and thousands of decisions per second. The Kria AI SoMs are due from ODM partners in the fourth quarter of 2026, while the developer platform is already sampling with early-access customers and is also scheduled for broader availability in the same quarter.
