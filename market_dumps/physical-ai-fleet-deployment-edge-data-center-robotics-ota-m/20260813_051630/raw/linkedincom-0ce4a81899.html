    While much of the attention in US autonomy remains focused on robotaxis and autonomous trucking, some of the most interesting progress is happening away from public roads. Fruition Group US have been fortunate enough to build autonomy teams across mining, construction, defense, agriculture and industrial logistics, where there is a continuous trend of innovation across physical AI and autonomous machines. The ecosystem's evolution has allowed for some organizations to gain strong traction, including some of the organizations we are watching closely: • Kodiak AI are expanding beyond highway autonomy into industrial and defense applications, including autonomous trucking operations in the Permian Basin. • Overland AI are certainly one of the most interesting players in defense autonomy, developing off-road autonomous systems for military ground vehicles. • FieldAI now focus on developing general-purpose autonomy for unstructured environments, with applications spanning defense, construction, mining and industrial robotics. • Komatsu North America – Demonstrating what mature commercial autonomy can look like, with autonomous haul trucks already operating at significant scale. The interesting part is the convergence, where Perception + AI + planning + controls + fleet orchestration are becoming one technology stack that can potentially power everything from autonomous haul trucks to military UGVs and construction equipment. Building reliable autonomy outside of structured environments requires a relatively rare combination of expertise across computer vision, perception, sensor fusion, machine learning, planning, controls, robotics, embedded systems and safety-critical software. The talent pool is already highly competitive, particularly for engineers who have taken autonomous systems from R&D into real-world deployment. As more defense, industrial and robotics companies enter the space, I expect competition for experienced autonomy engineers to become even more intense.
