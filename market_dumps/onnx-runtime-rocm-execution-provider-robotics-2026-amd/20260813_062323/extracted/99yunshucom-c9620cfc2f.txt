Current State of AMD's AI Business (2024–2025)
AMD's AI revenue has grown from near zero in 2022 to an estimated $6.5B in 2024, largely driven by the MI300X accelerator. Key customers include Microsoft, Meta, and Oracle. However, NVIDIA still commands ~80% of the data center AI market. AMD's Instinct line offers competitive price/performance, but software ecosystem gaps remain a barrier.
The company's roadmap includes the MI350 (2025) and MI400 (2026). The MI400 is expected to leverage 3nm process and advanced packaging, targeting HBM4 memory. AMD has also acquired Nod.ai and Mipsology to bolster software capabilities.
Key Factors Shaping the AMD AI 2026 Outlook
Product Roadmap and Performance
The MI400 series, scheduled for late 2025/early 2026, is expected to deliver 2x performance per watt over MI300X. If AMD can deliver on time, it will be competitive with NVIDIA's Blackwell Ultra and Rubin architectures. Delays could severely impact the outlook.
Software Ecosystem (ROCm)
ROCm adoption is critical. Currently, only ~70% of popular AI models run on ROCm without modification, vs. 95%+ on CUDA. AMD aims to reach 90% compatibility by 2026. Investments in open-source libraries and partnerships with Hugging Face are positive signals.
Customer Adoption and Supply Chain
AMD has secured design wins with major cloud providers. Microsoft reportedly uses MI300X for internal workloads. However, supply constraints (CoWoS packaging) have limited shipments. AMD's supply is expected to improve by 2026 as new packaging capacity comes online.
Expert Consensus and Historical Patterns
Analysts from firms like Gartner and IDC project the AI chip market to grow from $70B in 2024 to $160B by 2026. AMD's share is widely expected to rise, but estimates vary from 15% to 25%. Historical patterns from the CPU market show AMD can gain share when it delivers superior price/performance, as with EPYC processors (from 5% server share in 2017 to 30%+ in 2024).
