Meta Platforms deploys autonomous robots across data center construction and operations
From 72-ton pile drivers to rack-moving bots, Meta is betting that robots can solve the labor crunch threatening its $50B+ Hyperion project.
Meta Platforms is putting robots to work inside and around its data centers, deploying autonomous machines for everything from pounding steel beams into the ground to scanning server racks. The effort spans both the construction and day-to-day operations of Meta’s sprawling data infrastructure, with the most dramatic example being ten 72-ton robotic pile drivers now operating at a solar site tied to the company’s massive Hyperion AI data center campus in Louisiana.
The autonomous pile drivers, deployed by Built Robotics in July 2026, can drive nearly 1,000 steel beams per day. That output reduces the need for manual labor by roughly three to four times compared to traditional methods. Human workers haven’t been eliminated from the equation, but their role has shifted: they now serve as “robot foremen,” supervising autonomous equipment rather than doing the heavy lifting themselves.
The scale of Hyperion demands automation
Meta’s Hyperion project, located in Richland Parish, Louisiana, is one of the most ambitious data center builds in tech history. The campus is targeting up to 5 GW of compute capacity, a figure that has grown from an initial projection of 2 GW when the project was first announced in 2024. The estimated cost exceeds $50B.
The autonomous pile drivers operate for more than 12 hours daily, even in conditions that would slow human crews considerably, including knee-deep mud.
The construction robotics deployment is just one layer of Meta’s broader automation strategy. The company’s Site Engineering Robotics Team has been developing and deploying mobile robots for operational tasks inside data centers for years, with initiatives dating back to at least 2016.
Inside the data center: racks, scans, and autonomy
Beyond the construction site, Meta has introduced autonomous guided vehicles designed for heavy rack movements within its data centers. These robots handle the physically demanding work of positioning server racks, a task that traditionally requires multiple workers and carries injury risk.
Meta has also deployed robots for asset scanning, automating the tedious process of inventorying and monitoring the thousands of components that make up a modern hyperscale data center.
A broader industry shift
Meta isn’t alone in this push. Microsoft, Google, and other hyperscale operators are all exploring automation for data center construction and operations.
For investors tracking the hyperscale data center buildout, Meta’s robotics deployment signals something important about the economics of AI infrastructure. Companies like Built Robotics, which supplied the autonomous pile drivers, stand to benefit as every major cloud and AI infrastructure player confronts the same workforce limitations.
