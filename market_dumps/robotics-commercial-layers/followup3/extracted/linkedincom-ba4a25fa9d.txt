I wrote an article on substack not too long ago talking about a robot licensing framework that calls for estimating how many people the robot replaces. In this particular case, it is pretty straightforward.
R = operational robot hours
T = Average hours on job per trucker
B = Average non productive work hours (like breaks) per trucker
Humans Replaced = R/(T-B)
Others might be more complicated to calculate but in the end it is mostly about statistics tracking which we already have some infrastructure for.

Rethinking Humanoids: Our perspective on industrial robots 🤖
The robotics industry is seeing renewed debate on the real-world viability of humanoid robots for industrial automation and material handling. Experts ranging from global forums like the IEEE to thought leaders such as Rodney Brooks are questioning traditional notions of humanoids in advanced robotics applications.
At Ati Motors, we believe industrial robots should solve real challenges faced by factories and warehouses rather than mimic human form for its own sake. Inspiration from nature is valuable, just as aircraft are inspired by birds, but for moving heavy materials efficiently across industrial environments, efficiency and specialized engineering must come first.
In other words, we design robots that go beyond human capabilities to maximize productivity in logistics and autonomous electric vehicle deployments.
A fun parallel: In Star Wars, R2-D2 (the workhorse) reliably saves the day, while C-3PO (the humanoid) often needs rescuing, a lesson for robotics design! For the future of industrial automation, our view is clear: robots, whether humanoid or human-inspired, should be built as workhorses, focusing on outperforming humans in reliability and throughput rather than replacing or imitating us.
